    P/02
    Concept
    Margin Quarterly · Issue 07Essay

    The quiet grid is the loud one.

    Why the best-looking pages spend most of their effort on things you never notice.

    Words: Margin desk · 9 min

    Open any page you trust and measure it. The margins are wider than they need to be, the columns narrower, the headline smaller than your first instinct. None of it is an accident.

    A grid is a promise to the reader that the next thing will be where they expect it. Keep that promise and you can afford one surprise per spread.

    Break it every few inches and the surprise stops working.

    Magazine opener — Instrument Serif + ManropeA narrow serif buys editorial weight in very little width, so the headline sits in two lines; a neutral grotesk keeps the deck, byline and body out of its way.

    05 / 05
    House rules
    Four rules cover most of what makes a page feel typeset rather than typed. Each one below is live: change it and watch the page react.
    
  
    
    
    
      
    Rule 1 · ScaleOne ratio, every size
    
      
    
      
    Sizes come from a base of 16 px multiplied by one ratio. We use 1.25 for content-heavy sites and 1.333 or more when the page is mostly headline. Random sizes are the fastest way to make a page look template-made.
    
    
    
    
    
      
    Rule 2 · Measure60 to 75 characters
    Line: 68 ch
    
      
    60–75
    
      
    Long lines make the eye lose its place on the return; short ones break the sentence into a stutter. Somewhere between sixty and seventy-five characters, reading stops being work. Drag the slider and watch the count.
    
      
    
    
    
    
    
      
    Rule 3 · TrackingBigger type, tighter set
    
      
    
        
    14 px
    +0.01 em
    Field Kettle 02
    
        
    40 px
    −0.02 em
    Field Kettle 02
    
        
    84 px
    −0.045 em
    Kettle
    
      
    
      
    Fonts are spaced for reading sizes. Past about 40 px the gaps look loose, so display lines lose 2 to 5 percent of an em. Small caps and labels go the other way.
    
    
    
    
    
      
    Rule 4 · NumbersTabular in tables, proportional in prose
    
      
    
        
    Proportional
    Mon1,111.10
    Tue8,048.00
    Wed711.71
    Thu19,101.11
    
        
    Tabular
    Mon1,111.10
    Tue8,048.00
    Wed711.71
    Thu19,101.11
    
      
    
      
    Fractions off1/2 cup, 3/4 tsp
    Fractions on1/2 cup, 3/4 tsp
    
      
    Ones in proportional figures are narrow, so columns drift. Tables, prices and timers get tabular figures; running text keeps proportional ones. Recipes get real fractions. All set in Manrope.
