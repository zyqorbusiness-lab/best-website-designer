    01

    The brief

    What the studio needed, in one breath.

    Kilnfold fires forty pieces at a time. Each firing sells out in hours, mostly to whoever messages first, and everyone else finds out from a “sold” comment. The site has to make a sell-out feel fair, and make the wait for the next kiln part of the product.

    Problem

    Sales run through DMs. No stock count, no queue, and a lot of “is this still available?”

    Constraint

    Two potters, no developer. Every page has to update from one sheet of firing data.

    The bet

    Sell by firing, not by category: one dated drop, a visible count, and a waitlist that rolls into the next kiln.

    07

    Concept: what we would measure

    Kilnfold is not real and nothing launched, so there are no results on this page.

    These are the numbers we would agree with the studio before launch, each tied to a design decision made to move it. Targets, not outcomes.

    GoalDesign decisionWhat we would measure
    01Checkout stepsSix screens become three: pieces, details, pay. Address fills from the PIN code; guest checkout by default.Drop-to-paid time per order; drop-off at each step.
    02“Is this still available?”A live count on every piece. Sold pieces leave the grid within a second instead of staying up with a grey badge.Direct messages per firing, compared with the DM-only firings before launch.
    03Missed buyersOne tap moves you from a sold-out Firing 07 to the front of Firing 08, with a reminder the day before.Share of the waitlist that buys in the next firing.
    04Phone weightA 1.5 MB budget on phones; photos in WebP at two sizes; the countdown is HTML, not video.Largest Contentful Paint on a mid-range Android over 4G.
    05AccessAll three colours pass WCAG AA in their roles; the whole checkout works from a keyboard.An automated audit plus one screen-reader pass before each firing opens.
